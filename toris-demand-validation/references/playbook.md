# Demand evidence and a bounded pilot

Research basis: R01-R05 in [evidence.json](../../research/solo-developer-2026-10-15/evidence.json). These procedures are authored synthesis, not a claim that an interview or pilot was executed. Read source limitations before borrowing a benchmark.

## Recover the decision

Inspect the existing product brief, release state, support themes and any supplied research. Do not browse personal messages or contact customers unless the task authorizes that source/action. Pick one consequential uncertainty: problem severity, reachability, task success, commitment, delivery effort, or repeat use. Keep separate rows rather than turning them into an unexplained weighted score.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Customer and trigger | Who has the problem and the situation in which it matters |
| Existing evidence | Source, date, actual event and consent/privacy boundary |
| Workaround | What they actually do, including time, cost or abandonment |
| Uncertainty | What could invalidate the current choice |
| Next decision | Continue, change, stop, or collect a specific missing observation |
| Experiment | The smallest action that distinguishes the alternatives |
| Boundary | Authorized cost, founder capacity, explicit exclusions and review condition |

GOV.UK guidance asks research rounds to serve an explicit question and uses neutral interviews grounded in real examples ([S01](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-round-of-user-research), [S02](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews)). The business gates above are this package's adaptation, not government commercial advice.

## Interview guide, not invented interviews

Ask about a recent occasion: what triggered it, what they did, where it broke, what it cost them, and what happened next. Ask permission before recording and minimize personal data. Do not pitch first and then count agreeable answers. Do not assign a numeric prevalence from a convenience sample.

For each note distinguish participant report, observed action, researcher interpretation and unresolved question. A participant ID can be a local pseudonym; names and contact details do not belong in a shared skill. With no actual participants, produce only a clearly labeled discussion guide.

## Minimal pilot contract

Write the promised outcome, manual steps, customer inputs, delivery boundary, failure handling and founder effort. An honest limited pilot can test a service outcome without a complete platform. Do not pretend unfinished features exist, collect a real payment without permission, or manufacture scarcity.

A task prototype checks whether people can use the proposed interaction; that is different from whether they would buy it ([S03](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing)). Keep problem, usability, commitment, payment, repeat use and delivery economics as distinct columns.

Track cash expenses and time separately. Reconcile gross/net fee treatment before estimating contribution. One successful delivery might be unrepeatable without substantial manual effort; put that risk into the decision instead of hiding it behind automation plans.

## Benchmark comparison checklist

Before using B01/B02, match metric, observation horizon, statistic and population. The selected RevenueCat report is observational and targets 2025 data from eligible integrated subscription apps ([S04](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). It is not a random sample of all solo attempts. A difference between groups is not the effect of switching this product's access model. The quarantined store comparisons are not usable inputs.
