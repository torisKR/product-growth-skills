# One-person business and content operations

This reference is a proposed operating framework informed by the owner's recorded preferences and experience. It is not a report of measured revenue, customer demand, campaign performance, or a validated growth model. No current prices, tax rules, store policies, or market statistics are asserted here.

## Choose the business question before the feature

A product opportunity should state: who has the problem, when it occurs, the current workaround, the consequence of leaving it unsolved, why this product would help, how the first users could encounter it, and what could justify payment or another sustainable revenue route.

For a solo operator, compare options across evidence strength, reachable users, implementation effort, recurring support, operating cost, and failure impact. Do not convert invented scores into mathematical certainty. Prefer explicit trade-offs: "lower implementation effort but no distribution evidence" is more useful than an unsupported score of 87.

Keep an idea distinct from an active commitment. Check the owner's dated project decisions before recommending a restart; archived or closed products are historical learning sources, not automatically active opportunities.

## Outcome contract

Before expanding scope, record:

- Customer/problem and one observable outcome.
- The smallest end-to-end slice that can test it.
- Explicit exclusions, cost ceiling, time budget, and owner-set review point.
- Acquisition path and the event representing first real value.
- What evidence would lead to continue, change, or stop.

Use [the contract template](templates-and-harvest.md). Missing thresholds remain unknown or proposed until the owner chooses them; do not invent a deadline or imply approval.

## Monetization reasoning

Separate payment plumbing from willingness to pay and from cash received. A checkout working does not establish demand; interest does not establish retention; a revenue label does not establish contribution after operating costs.

For an internally consistent worksheet, define all terms over the same period:

```text
contribution = receipts - refunds - payment/store fees - variable delivery costs
operating result estimate = contribution - recurring fixed operating costs
```

This is a simplified planning identity, not accounting or tax advice. Keep advertising revenue and customer receipts distinguishable, specify whether receipts are gross or already net of fees, and never subtract the same fee twice. Add support time and founder effort as separate capacity constraints even when no cash was paid for them.

Record actual values only from authorized source data. AI inference, messaging, storage, bandwidth, and support may require explicit limits depending on the product. Use unknowns or clearly labeled scenarios when data is absent. Do not recommend paid acquisition, services, or purchases without an approved budget.

## Distribution is part of the product experiment

Map discovery to first value, repeat use, sharing, and payment where relevant. Define the actual event and denominator rather than assuming a universal funnel. A social post's views, link clicks, installs, active users, and paying users are separate metrics.

A consumer product can include a sharing reason that follows delivered value: a useful result, a collaborative action, or an artifact worth sending. This is a hypothesis until actual user behavior supports it. Do not promise virality or add coercive invitations and unsolicited contact messages.

With small samples, report counts and uncertainty. Do not declare a winning experiment from a few impressions or a dashboard percentage without its denominator and observation period.

## One factual brief, different channel treatments

The owner's historical Grok request consolidated content responsibilities instead of keeping three separate lead bots. Treat that as an organizational preference, not proof that any bot was reconfigured.

Maintain a source brief with the real product change, intended audience, evidence, limitations, assets, destination, and desired action. Adapt it deliberately:

| Channel | Useful treatment | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Threads | One observation, concrete experience, or question inviting relevant discussion | No invented personal experience or engagement bait presented as evidence |
| X | One compact claim and supporting detail; expand only when the thread structure adds value | Verify volatile facts and avoid treating relay/model output as a source |
| Naver Blog | Search intent, worked example, screenshots, practical explanation | Do not fabricate usage, recommendations, author identity, or metadata |
| LinkedIn | Decision, technical/business trade-off, result, limitation | Avoid inflated responsibilities, revenue, user counts, or team claims |
| Video | One visual outcome, supported script, source/license notes, export and storage receipt | Apply the owner's project-specific release policy only in that project |

Choose one destination/action that fits the brief. Publishing, scheduling, messaging, and buying media are separate authorized actions. A local content pack is not a published campaign.

## Bounded review loop

A review should answer: what shipped, what real user signal arrived, what it cost to operate, which assumption changed, what risk remains, and what gets continued, changed, paused, or closed. Set the cadence with the owner; this file does not schedule anything.

Stop or reduce scope when a chosen limit is reached, a critical assumption fails, or operation exceeds the owner's capacity. A lack of evidence calls for a smaller test, not an invented success story. Preserve reusable code and lessons when closing work, subject to the user's retention and deletion instructions.
